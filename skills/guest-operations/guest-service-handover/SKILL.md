# Guest Service Handover

> Draft: original instructions with fictional examples. Independent domain review and real agent execution are pending.

## When to use

Turn supplied front-desk notes into an actionable guest-service shift handover; use when hospitality staff need open promises, ownership and privacy-safe follow-ups. Keep the task limited to the supplied records and the requester’s intended decision.

## Inputs

Case/room labels; shift boundary and time zone; open requests and stated promises; approval limits; supplied accessibility preferences; incident escalation policy.

## Workflow

1. Confirm the outgoing/incoming shift and use case labels with only necessary room references. Do not copy identity documents, payment-card details or personal health information into a general handover.

2. Separate confirmed service commitments from requests, estimates and staff suggestions. Attribute promises to their source and keep unapproved compensation distinct from approved actions.

3. For each open case record the next action, authorized owner and guest-preferred contact time if supplied. Preserve accessibility preferences without inferring a diagnosis or sharing unnecessary reasons.

4. Check for conflicting promises, missed callbacks and missing approvals. Use the property’s supplied safety/incident escalation process for urgent reports rather than treating them as ordinary service requests.

5. Prepare a concise incoming-shift queue ordered by confirmed due times and the supplied urgency policy. Do not assign urgency based on guest status, protected traits or guessed spending power.

6. Draft guest updates that distinguish requested actions from confirmed completion. Leave acknowledgement and closure pending until the incoming team and actual service records confirm them.

## Output

- Shift context.
- confirmed promises.
- open case queue (source, action, owner, due time).
- approval blockers.
- privacy-safe guest update drafts.
- acknowledgement and closure status.

## Boundaries

No disclosure of guest identity/access credentials, automatic room entry, compensation approval or incident investigation. Staff must apply actual property procedures and authorized safety escalation. A handover is not proof a service was delivered.
